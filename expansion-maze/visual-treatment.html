Fisherman & Trout

Tone

We’re drawing from the look of Birth, The Shining, Rosemary’s Baby, Funny Games, Safe, Taxi Driver. These are films that invite us into the internal minds of their heroes while presenting a bleak, observational, expressive view of an impossible situation.

They ground their surrealism and convince us that these are real people simply dealing with something they can’t explain. Our goal is to make the audience feel what Jon feels — trapped, suspicious, unable to trust the people closest to them.

Camera

Our camera should feel mechanical. It’s always controlled movement or completely static. It never moves handheld. We’re isolating Jon from everyone around him, creating a me vs. them atmosphere.

Jealousy, distrust, fear. Our characters rarely speak their feelings, but they’re incredibly present throughout the film. Our camera needs to find those moments and convey them to the audience without words.

Production Design

We want to lean into the jagged edges, the broken walls, the plastic tarps covering the furniture. This movie wants you to feel uneasy, and the production design of a house under construction should do exactly that. Mess, clutter, boxes, leaking roofs.

Music

We want something abrasive and atonal, built from drones and dissonant strings that sit underneath every scene like something is wrong. The music should feel almost industrial — as if it were built from the sounds of construction.

Sound Design

We want the soundscape to have a big dynamic range. We keep things quiet so the loud moments feel violent. An impossibly quiet corridor… completely silent. Then a gunshot so loud it rings through the rest of the scene.

When we enter the maze, the soundscape shifts entirely. The outside world disappears. What’s left is Jon’s breathing, his footsteps, and a liminal droning sound.

Edit

We take our time. Shots linger. Scenes breathe. The audience doesn’t get the relief of a cut when they want one. We hold on faces too long. We let silence do the work.

When we do cut, the cuts are hard. The edit mirrors Jon’s psychology. Early on, the rhythm is steady. As Jon deteriorates, the cuts become disorienting.
