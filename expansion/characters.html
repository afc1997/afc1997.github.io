Fisherman & Trout

Jon has spent his entire life running from his past. Smart, loyal, and secretly insecure. When he inherits his father’s house, there’s nowhere left to run. Contained, until it isn’t.

Esme is grounded where Jon is not. She believes everything can be fixed — until she can’t anymore. Warmth and maternal care on the surface, something hidden underneath.

Parker is everything Jon wishes he could be — effortlessly charismatic, confident, physically imposing. His golden retriever energy seems friendly, but underneath it might be calculated.

Richard hangs himself in the first scene — but his presence never leaves. Brilliant and cruel. To the world, a genius. To Jon, a monster.

The house is an extension of Richard's mind. Monumental, isolated, fully wrecked. A character in its own right, one carrying a secret within its walls.

Unlike the house, the maze is always in pristine condition. Its walls plaster white, incredibly tall, endlessly long. We never know what’s waiting behind a turn.
